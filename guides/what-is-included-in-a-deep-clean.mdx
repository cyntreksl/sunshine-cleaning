## Start with the outcome

A deep clean is not a universal checklist. The useful question is: **which build-up, rooms and details need more attention than a routine clean allows?** Agreeing that first gives the cleaner a realistic scope and gives you a clear way to check the result.

## Kitchen priorities

Typical priorities include grease on reachable surfaces, cupboard fronts, splashbacks, sink fittings, external appliance surfaces, edges, skirting boards and detailed floor cleaning. Inside appliances or cupboards should be named separately; never assume they are included.

Remove food, paperwork and fragile items from the work areas you want cleaned. Clearing surfaces does not make the home look artificially tidy—it gives the cleaner more time for the work you are paying for.

## Bathroom priorities

Bathrooms often need extra time for limescale, soap residue, grout lines, shower screens, taps, fixtures, mirrors and the edges around sanitaryware. Heavy staining, damaged sealant and mould within materials may not be removable through ordinary cleaning, so point these out before the quote.

## Living areas and bedrooms

The deeper work is usually around ledges, reachable frames, switches, skirting boards, furniture edges, detailed vacuuming and hard floors. Moving heavy furniture is a separate safety and scope decision.

## Before you book

Share the approximate property size, current condition, occupied or empty status, priority rooms, access and preferred date. Photographs can help a cleaner assess the scope, but avoid sending anything private that is not relevant to the work.

The best deep-clean checklist is short enough to complete properly and specific enough that nobody has to guess.
