## Frequency follows use

There is no honest single answer for every office. A small quiet studio and a busy shared workplace create different cleaning needs. Start by mapping people, footfall, food, washrooms and client-facing areas.

## Daily or high-frequency tasks

Bins, shared kitchens, washrooms, entrances and obvious spill risks often need the most frequent attention. High-touch points may also matter more where many people share the same space.

## Weekly routine

Desk surfaces that are clear and included in the scope, meeting rooms, internal glass spot cleaning, vacuuming and suitable hard floors can form the core of a regular schedule. The exact split depends on occupancy and working patterns.

## Less frequent detail work

Ledges, reachable frames, skirting boards, detailed internal glass and low-use storage areas may fit a rotating or periodic plan. Scheduling them deliberately prevents them disappearing from the routine altogether.

## A simple way to set the schedule

1. List every room and shared area.
2. Give each a low, medium or high usage rating.
3. Identify hygiene-sensitive or client-facing areas.
4. Agree daily, weekly and rotating tasks.
5. Review the plan after staffing or office use changes.

A clear schedule protects cleaning time for the areas that actually affect the working day, instead of applying the same frequency to everything.
